\begin{abstract}
Simulation-pretrained latent world models with sampling-based planners can be adapted to a real legged robot from
minutes of data by finetuning only the latent dynamics. We ask what this adaptation can and cannot fix. On a
Unitree Go1 and a matched simulation-to-simulation benchmark (perturbed actuation, payload and friction), we find
three things. (1) \emph{Coverage is set by the planner's prior alone}: under a 60\,\% weaker actuator, neither
planning on the pretrained model nor dynamics adaptation from 45k real-format steps reduces falls when the prior
(the cloned policy) was not trained to cover that regime (\prelim{10 falls per 1000 steps for every prior whose
randomization stops above $0.7\times$ nominal torque}), while widening the prior's randomization to cover it
cuts falls fourfold with no real data. The covering prior does not identify the actuator regime from its
history (probe $R^2\approx0$); it is a single robust gait. (2) \emph{Planning recovers the prior's imperfection
inside its coverage}: on top of every prior the planner halves to quarters the falls of the cloned policy and
raises reward, with the largest gains for the least accurate priors. (3) \emph{Adaptation data is coupled to the
prior that collected it}: dynamics adapted on data from the narrow prior make the planner with the broad prior
\prelim{2.2$\times$} worse than the pretrained model, whereas data collected by the deployed prior never hurt
and improve the nominal condition. These results give a simple recipe --- cover the target regime in simulation
first, plan on top of the covering prior, and collect adaptation data with that prior --- which we validate on
the Go1 under software torque caps and a payload. \todo{hardware numbers}
\end{abstract}

\section{Limitations}
Inside the covered regime the planner on a broad prior matches the broad policy alone; the value of planning
lies in recovering imperfect priors and, untested here, in new objectives and constraints. Adaptation gave no
gain at $0.4\times$ torque because the pretrained dynamics were already adequate around the robust prior. Single
embodiment; one expert seed per width (\todo{update with seeds}); falls and reward as the only metrics;
sim-to-sim shifts are physically simple (actuation, payload, friction). The label-free diagnostic of coverage
we attempted (history visibility) failed; the practical test remains ``does the prior walk there in simulation''.
