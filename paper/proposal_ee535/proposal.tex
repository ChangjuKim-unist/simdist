\section{Problem}
SimDist~\cite{simdist} pretrains a latent world model in simulation and controls a quadruped with a sampling planner (MPPI) that imagines hundreds of action sequences and executes the one the model scores best. On the real robot the model is never exactly right, and a planner is the worst consumer of a wrong model: it searches for the highest score, so it preferentially picks candidates whose score is \emph{inflated} by model error. Existing remedies widen simulation coverage (SimDist's recipe) or penalise uncertainty, which makes the robot stop rather than fall less. This project asks: \emph{can the planner tell, from its own recent prediction errors, when its imagination is trustworthy, and plan only then?}

\section{Preliminary measurements}
Sim-to-sim benchmark (Isaac Lab, Go1, 6 seeds $\times$ 3 speeds, 100\,s episodes); metric: falls per 1000 control steps (lower is better).
(1)~\textbf{Planning helps inside the model's coverage and hurts outside it.} With a world model fine-tuned on broad domain randomisation (motor 0.3--1.1, friction 0.2--1.2), prior-only vs.\ planning gives $0.46 \to 0.20$ falls at motor 0.4 (inside coverage) but $0.03 \to 0.47$ at friction 0.15 (outside; paired 95\% CI $[0.09, 0.81]$, progress $55 \to 33$\,m), all extra falls at 0.9\,m/s. Writing the planner's gain as \emph{promised gain} minus \emph{selection bias} predicts this (the bias grows with the spread of the return error: $0.02$ at 0.3\,m/s, $0.16$ at 0.9\,m/s).
(2)~\textbf{The model's own errors are persistent and predictive.} One-step latent errors have autocorrelation 0.88/0.58/0.42 at lags 1/25/100 steps; the mean error over the last 25 steps correlates $-0.64$ with the return the model over-promises next ($n{=}88$k).
(3)~\textbf{Real-data adaptation trades axes.} Dynamics-only adaptation on low-friction data fixes friction ($0.59 \to 0.17$) but triples falls at motor 0.4 ($0.27 \to 0.77$): the model becomes right on one axis and wrong on another.

\section{Method}
The SimDist core (encoder, history encoder, dynamics, reward, value, prior $\pi$) stays \emph{frozen}; three components are added.
\textbf{(a) Self-error context.} At step $t$ the robot holds the last 25 one-step prediction errors $e_s = \hat z_{s+1|s} - z_{s+1}$ and $\hat r_s - r_s$ (imagined minus realised); a small encoder maps this window to context tokens $\epsilon_t$. Unlike RMA-style encoders that estimate physics, $\epsilon_t$ describes \emph{how the model is currently wrong}.
\textbf{(b) Corrected return head.} Given an imagined trajectory, its promised return $\hat J$ and $\epsilon_t$, a head outputs quantiles of the \emph{realised} 25-step return; the planner scores candidates with a lower quantile instead of $\hat J$.
\textbf{(c) Plan-on-demand gate.} Each step the prior's own action chunk is imagined and scored by (b); if its corrected score is safe the prior is executed without planning, otherwise MPPI runs with corrected scores and its action is blended with the prior in proportion to the predicted improvement.
\textbf{Training.} The frozen model is run in deliberately mismatched simulated physics (motor, friction, payload per episode; half prior-only, half planning), recording each step's imagined latents, rewards and values for the executed plan and what actually happened. Realised returns and falls are free labels; (a)+(b) are trained by quantile regression on them with one axis held out. No weights change at deployment.

\section{Experiments and metrics}
\textbf{Simulation (main).} Conditions: nominal, motor 0.4/0.5, friction 0.15, payload 3\,kg, a 0.4$\to$0.8 motor switch and a speed-step command, for a narrow and a broad pretrained model, zero-shot and after real-data adaptation. Arms: prior-only, MPPI, MPPI+(b) without error context, MPPI+(b), gate (c), an ensemble-uncertainty penalty (baseline), and the norm-threshold gate that failed in our earlier work. Metrics: falls per 1000 steps (paired bootstrap CI over 18 runs), reward per step, forward progress, tracking error, planning fraction $\rho$ (latency $\approx 48\rho + 3(1-\rho)$\,ms on an RTX~3060). Pre-registered gates: the error window predicts a fall within 100 steps with AUC $\ge 0.8$ and beats a raw state-history probe by $\ge 0.1$ (else (a) is rejected); the gate is within CI of the better of prior-only and MPPI in every condition and better than both in at least one.
\textbf{Hardware (Nov.\ 10--24).} Real Go1: nominal, PTFE sheet, 3\,kg payload; prior-only vs.\ planner vs.\ gate, 10 trials each, before and after 30 minutes of real-data adaptation. Metrics: success over a 10\,m course, progress, tracking error, $\rho$; pre-registered prediction = the pattern of measurement~(1).
