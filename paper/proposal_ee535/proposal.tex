\section{Problem}
SimDist~\cite{simdist} pretrains a latent world model (encoder, history-conditioned dynamics, reward, value, policy prior) in simulation and controls a quadruped with a sampling planner (MPPI) that imagines hundreds of candidate action sequences and executes the one the model scores best. The model is never exactly right on the real robot, and a planner is the worst consumer of a wrong model: it searches for the highest score, so it preferentially picks candidates whose score is \emph{inflated} by model error. Existing remedies either widen simulation coverage (SimDist's own recipe) or penalise uncertain predictions, which in our experience makes the robot stop rather than fall less. This project asks: \emph{can the planner tell, from its own recent prediction errors, when its imagination is trustworthy, and plan only then?}

\section{What we have measured so far}
Sim-to-sim benchmark (Isaac Lab, Go1, 6 seeds $\times$ 3 speeds, 100\,s episodes); metric: falls per 1000 control steps (lower is better); physical shifts: motor strength and foot friction.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Planning helps inside the model's coverage and hurts outside it.} With a world model fine-tuned on broad domain randomisation (motor 0.3--1.1, friction 0.2--1.2), executing the policy prior alone vs.\ planning gives, at motor 0.4 (inside coverage), $0.46 \to 0.20$ falls (planning helps; reward higher with CI excluding 0), but at friction 0.15 (outside coverage), $0.03 \to 0.47$ (planning hurts; 95\% CI of the paired difference $[0.09, 0.81]$, forward progress $55 \to 33$\,m). All extra falls occur at 0.9\,m/s. Decomposing the planner's gain as \emph{promised gain} minus \emph{selection bias} predicts exactly this: the bias scales with the spread of the model's return error, which we measure as $0.02$ at 0.3\,m/s and $0.16$ at 0.9\,m/s. (Preliminary, from this week.)
\item \textbf{The model's own prediction errors are persistent and predictive.} One-step latent prediction errors have autocorrelation 0.88/0.58/0.42 at lags 1/25/100 steps, and the mean error over the last 25 steps correlates $-0.64$ with the return the model will over-promise in the next 25 steps ($n{=}88$k windows). Errors rise from 1.02 to 1.67 in the 100 steps before a fall.
\item \textbf{Real-data adaptation trades one axis for another.} Adapting only the dynamics on low-friction data (SimDist's recipe) fixes friction ($0.59 \to 0.17$) but triples falls at motor 0.4 ($0.27 \to 0.77$): after adaptation the model is right on one axis and wrong on another, which is precisely where a trust signal is needed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Plan for the course project}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Trust signal (simulation).} Feed the last 25 one-step prediction errors (imagined minus realised latent and reward) to a small encoder whose output conditions a return-correction head trained on \emph{self-rollouts}: the pretrained model is run in deliberately mismatched physics and its realised 25-step returns and falls are recorded as free labels. The world model itself stays frozen. Pre-registered test: the error window must predict falls within 100 steps with AUC $\ge 0.8$ and beat a raw state-history baseline by $\ge 0.1$; otherwise the idea is rejected.
\item \textbf{Plan-on-demand gate.} Execute the prior when the corrected score of the prior's own imagined rollout is safe; plan (with corrected scores) only when it is not. Evaluate prior-only / planner / gate over motor, friction, payload and speed-change conditions, zero-shot and after real-data adaptation, with ablations (gate without error input, uncertainty penalty baseline).
\item \textbf{Hardware (Nov.\ 10--24).} Real Go1: nominal, PTFE sheet (low friction), 3\,kg payload; prior-only vs.\ planner vs.\ gate, 10 trials each, before and after 30 minutes of real-data adaptation, with the pattern of item~1 as the pre-registered prediction.
\end{enumerate}
Course material used: model-based RL and world models, sampling MPC (MPPI), uncertainty and calibration, domain randomisation and sim-to-real transfer.
